\begin{abstract}
A recognizer moved to a new corpus often has few labels per emotion besides labeled data from other corpora. We analyze what these labels repair in a linear classifier on frozen audio, face, and text features across pairs of seven corpus groups. Splitting each pairwise decision into a threshold and a direction shows that for single-modality audio and face with up to five labels per emotion, \NUM{p1-e29-critshare-kle5-single-lo} to \NUM{p1-e29-critshare-kle5-single-hi} of what the labels add to a pooled classifier comes from correcting thresholds, whereas audio directions improve at a rate set by an effective dimension of \NUM{p1-qdir-audio-lo} to \NUM{p1-qdir-audio-hi}, and one source corpus supplies \NUM{p1-e32-n1src-audio-lo} to \NUM{p1-e32-n1src-audio-hi} labels per emotion of direction. Neither few labels nor label-free selection repairs a strong start's direction. We therefore train an anchored linear student on weak labels that source-trained classifiers seeing modalities the student lacks assign to all unlabeled target utterances. At zero and five labels, this raises every single-modality student above the start and beats the strongest of nine identically tuned competitors in \NUM{cmp-ours-vs-best-pos-n} of \NUM{cmp-ours-vs-best-cells-n} cells; with teachers refit on the labels instead, an extended version beats them for all eleven students, second encoders included, by \NUM{cmp-oursx-vs-best-range-lo} to \NUM{cmp-oursx-vs-best-range-hi} UAR points.
\end{abstract}
