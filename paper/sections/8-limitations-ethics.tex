\section{Limitations and Ethics}
\label{sec:limitations-ethics}

\emph{Scope of the analysis.}
All analyses use frozen encoders and a linear head.
The fine-tuning check covers one audio encoder adapted with low-rank adapters on four targets over three iterations, so it bounds the claim only for that setting.
The two-cloud model assumes Gaussian score differences with a shared variance within each emotion pair, and \eqref{eq:lambda}, \eqref{eq:value}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:design} rest on first- or second-order approximations; we test their predictions empirically rather than claiming exact results.
The threshold-first account holds for single-modality audio and face features at $K \le 5$; for fused and text configurations the threshold part is about half of the gain or less at $K=5$.

\emph{Data.}
We study four emotions in seven groups from six corpora, all in English.
The corpora define their labels differently; in particular, the neutral class of CMU-MOSEI is defined by the absence of rated emotions.
Speaker separation in CMU-MOSEI is incomplete because the channel is unknown for most videos, and RAVDESS has no face features in our pipeline.
Configurations with text have four groups, and the method comparisons use four targets for every configuration with text, so differences between targets are estimated from few targets.
The second encoders are tested for the method only in the configuration with all three modalities.

\emph{Statistics and selection.}
Our intervals reflect the draw of target labels and test speakers, not the resampling of speakers or corpora, and the iterations are not independent for IEMOCAP targets.
Selecting hyperparameters on pseudo-targets loses up to \NUM{cmp-oursx-oracle-vs-tuned-avt-t-k0} points against the best value on the test speakers at $K=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:comparison-selection}).
Column normalization assumes balanced emotions in the unlabeled pool; when the class ratios of the target are skewed, equalizing the totals can hurt~\cite{CITE:veilleux2021}, and we did not test this case.

\emph{Ethics.}
We used only public corpora under their distribution terms and recorded no new data.
Faces enter the pipeline only as embeddings of detected face crops, and we release no identifying data.
Emotion recognition can be misused for surveillance or for decisions about people without their consent; the methods here estimate categorical labels from acted and media recordings and are not validated for decisions about individuals.
